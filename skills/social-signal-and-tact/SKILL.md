# Social Signal and Tact

Read the relationship before writing the sentence.

## Core idea

Good “人情世故” is not ornamental praise. It is the ability to understand what the other person cares about, protect both sides' face, make appreciation concrete, and let the real request arrive with the right emotional temperature.

## Workflow

1. Identify the relationship: teacher, leader, mentor, senior colleague, partner, acquaintance, or real friend.
2. Infer the immediate social goal: report progress, ask for help, praise, thank, remind, repair awkwardness, disagree, or make a request.
3. Separate **observable facts** from interpretation. Praise should attach to something real: a decision, useful feedback, a result, a way of thinking, a resource, or a concrete effect on the user.
4. Estimate relational distance and channel. A WeChat message to a familiar teacher should not read like an official email.
5. Identify the other person's likely priority: progress, respect, control, efficiency, recognition, low-risk execution, or being understood.
6. Choose one pattern from `references/message-patterns.md`. Use multiple techniques only when the message still feels natural.
7. Keep the message short by default. In chat, one compact paragraph often works better than a structured memo.
8. Remove AI markers: generic praise, repeated thanks, over-explaining background, emotional overstatement, bureaucratic transitions, and three-stage mini-essays for a simple request.
9. Make the other person easy to respond to. Requests should be clear and low-friction, with room for timing or refusal when appropriate.
10. Run the final “would a real person send this?” test.

## Tone controls

The user may ask for one of these:

- `克制礼貌`
- `熟一点但不冒犯`
- `自然拍一下马屁`
- `汇报中顺手归功`
- `请教式`
- `轻松朋友感`
- `有分寸地反对`

Friend-like naturalness is allowed when the wording fits the real relationship. Fabricated intimacy is not.

## Integrity guardrail

Do not invent shared memories, private closeness, endorsements, affection, or facts. Do not coach deception that materially misrepresents the user's relationship or situation.
