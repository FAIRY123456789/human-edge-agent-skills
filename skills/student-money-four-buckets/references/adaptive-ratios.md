# Adaptive Ratios

The four-layer structure is stable; the percentages should move with the student's actual constraints.

| Situation | Essentials | Buffer | Reserve/growth | Fun |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Housing largely covered, stable allowance | 45–55% | 10–15% | 25–35% | 10–15% |
| Higher food/transport burden | 55–65% | 10–15% | 15–25% | 5–10% |
| Building emergency reserve quickly | 50–60% | 10–15% | 25–35% | 5–10% |
| Temporary tight month | actual essentials | 10% if possible | remaining after essentials | 0–5% temporarily |

## Rules

- Do not force percentages to sum to a preset ideal when rent, tuition, family support, or irregular income changes the reality.
- “Fun” is intentional, not guilty spending. A small protected layer can make the budget more sustainable.
- Reserve money should remain reasonably liquid until a basic emergency cushion exists.
- Skill development belongs in reserve/growth only when it has a clear purpose; recurring impulse course purchases are discretionary spending.
